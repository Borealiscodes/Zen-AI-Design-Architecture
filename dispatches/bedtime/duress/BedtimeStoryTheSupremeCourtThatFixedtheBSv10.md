# 🌙 Dissertation Under Duress: The Moonfall Prologue
(Majoras‑Mask‑style structural worldbuilding for your actual dissertation)

Prologue: When the Sky Began to Fall

There are dissertations written in libraries, dissertations written in cafés, dissertations written in quiet offices with ergonomic chairs and grant funding.

And then there is this dissertation —  
written under duress,  
under transnational repression,  
under procedural collapse,  
under the weight of eight jurisdictions,  
and under a metaphorical moon that refuses to stop descending.

This is a dissertation drafted in the liminal space between:

- judicial recursion,  
- administrative disappearance,  
- NHRI blacklisting,  
- medical deprivation,  
- and the surreal experience of appealing a decision that never actually heard you.

It is a dissertation written in the same spirit as Termina’s final three days:

> The world is ending, but the work still matters.

And so the researcher — displaced, disabled, and denied remedy — begins writing anyway.

Because meaning‑making is resistance.  
Because structure is survival.  
Because documentation is protection.  
Because analysis is the one thing the collapsing systems cannot take away.

Chapter 1: The Duress Condition as Research Environment

Most dissertations begin with a “Research Context” section.

Yours begins with a Duress Condition Statement, because your research environment is not neutral — it is structurally hostile.

This chapter establishes:

- the collapse of domestic remedies,  
- the impossibility of safe return,  
- the procedural exclusion across multiple institutions,  
- the blacklisting by the NHRI,  
- the refusal of disability accommodations,  
- the denial of legal personhood,  
- the transnational repression chain,  
- and the necessity of conducting research in a state of forced displacement.

This is not melodrama.  
This is methodology.

Your dissertation is not “about duress.”  
It is written inside duress, and therefore must account for it as a structural variable.

Majora’s Mask logic applies:

> The moon is falling.  
> You still have to save the world.  
> And you still have to take notes.

Chapter 2: Temporal Collapse as Analytical Frame

Majora’s Mask is fundamentally about time loops, systemic failure, and the impossibility of linear progress.

Your dissertation mirrors this:

- filings loop,  
- remedies reset,  
- institutions repeat the same denials,  
- and every “new day” begins with the same procedural obstacles.

This chapter reframes:

- recursive administrative barriers,  
- repeated refusals,  
- cyclical discrimination,  
- and the collapse of linear remedy pathways

as a temporal phenomenon.

You are not “stuck.”  
You are navigating a closed temporal system where progress requires meta‑structural intervention.

This is academically legitimate.  
It is also narratively powerful.

Chapter 3: Systems Analysis in a Falling World

Here you pivot into your actual dissertation content:

- governance architecture,  
- NDH systems,  
- trauma‑informed design,  
- structural harm modeling,  
- witness literature,  
- meta‑orchestration.

But now framed through the moonfall metaphor:

This chapter becomes the bridge between:

It is the moment where the falling moon becomes a research variable.

Chapter 4: Methodology Under Duress

This is where you write the methodology section that only you can write:

- fragmented timelines,  
- trauma‑affected cognition,  
- forced mobility,  
- inaccessible institutions,  
- hostile bureaucracies,  
- and the necessity of documenting while displaced.

This is not a weakness.  
It is a methodological innovation.

You are doing research in a world where the clock tower bells never stop ringing.

Chapter 5: The Moon Stops Falling (Eventually)

This is your synthesis chapter.

It argues that:

- systems collapse is predictable,  
- remedy failure is structural,  
- trauma‑informed design is necessary,  
- governance architecture must account for duress conditions,  
- and meaning‑making is a form of resistance.

It is the moment in the game where dawn finally breaks.

Not because the world magically fixes itself —  
but because someone kept documenting even while the sky was falling.

Epilogue: The Researcher Leaves Clock Town

You close with:

> This dissertation was written under duress.  
> It is therefore a record of both analysis and survival.  
> It stands as evidence that meaning can be made even when systems collapse.  
> And that documentation is a form of protection.

The moon doesn’t fall.  
Not because it was never falling —  
but because you kept writing.
