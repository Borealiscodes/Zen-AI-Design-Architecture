## 🧊 2 — Emergency Triggers

A Snowflake Drift Emergency is declared when any of the following occur:

- **Jitter Spike** — lateral drift exceeds safe wobble amplitude.  
- **Gradient Snap** — sudden temperature shear causes drift acceleration.  
- **Branch Instability** — humidity shock destabilizes branching.  
- **Curvature Looping** — oscillation enters resonance amplification.  
- **Scatter Cascade** — turbulence pockets cause chaotic drift scatter.

Each trigger corresponds to a hazard zone in the  
**Snowflake Drift Hazard Map**.

## 🌬️ 3 — Emergency Response Sequence (ERS)

### **ERS‑1 — Stabilize Orientation**  
Align the snowflake’s center axis to reduce rotational wobble.

### **ERS‑2 — Reduce Drift Velocity**  
Apply micro‑drag through controlled descent vectors.

### **ERS‑3 — Branch Reinforcement Check**  
Ensure no branch is undergoing collapse or singularity formation.

### **ERS‑4 — Curvature Dampening**  
Introduce curvature‑neutral micro‑adjustments to prevent resonance loops.

### **ERS‑5 — Environmental Reassessment**  
Recalculate:

- turbulence intensity  
- temperature gradient  
- humidity variance  
- curvature amplification  

### **ERS‑6 — Safe Zone Relocation**  
Move the snowflake toward:

- Region A (Hexa‑Cluster)  
- Region C (Micro‑Environment Halo)  
- Region D (Curvature Stability Belt)  

depending on drift mode.
