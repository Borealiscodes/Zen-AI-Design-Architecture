## ⭐ 1 — Hazard Identity  
A snowflake drift hazard is defined as:

> **Any region of the cosmic manifold where a snowflake’s  
> drift becomes unstable due to intersecting turbulence,  
> curvature amplification, or environmental volatility.**

This is a *playful hazard model*, not NDH geometry.

---

## 🧊 2 — Hazard Factors (Primary)

Hazards emerge from four intersecting influences:

- **Turbulence Pockets** — chaotic micro‑eddies that amplify drift jitter.  
- **Temperature Shear Zones** — sudden gradients causing wobble spikes.  
- **Humidity Shock Regions** — rapid moisture changes destabilizing branches.  
- **Curvature Resonance Points** — local curvature amplifying drift oscillation.

These factors combine to create distinct hazard regions.

---

## 🌬️ 3 — Hazard Zones (NDH‑Safe)

### **Zone A — Turbulence Vortex Field**  
High jitter, unpredictable lateral drift, rotational wobble.

### **Zone B — Temperature Shear Corridor**  
Sharp gradient → sudden drift acceleration or deceleration.

### **Zone C — Humidity Shock Basin**  
Moisture variance → branching instability → drift wobble.

### **Zone D — Curvature Resonance Ring**  
Curvature amplification → oscillation loops → drift spirals.

These zones are playful analogues to NDH hazard envelopes.

function classifyHazard(snowflake):

    hazard.turbulenceRisk = measureTurbulence(snowflake.environment)
    hazard.temperatureRisk = measureGradient(snowflake.temperatureVariance)
    hazard.humidityRisk = measureMoistureDelta(snowflake.humidityVariance)
    hazard.curvatureRisk = measureCurvatureAmplification(snowflake.curvature)
