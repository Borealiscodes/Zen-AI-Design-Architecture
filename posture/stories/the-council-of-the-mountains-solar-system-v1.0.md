# 🌌 **Story: *The Council of the Mountains***  
### *A solar‑system myth told like an old legend whispered by starlight*

Long before humans named planets, long before telescopes blinked awake, long before the first rover rolled across Martian dust, the mountains of the solar system held a council.

They met once every thousand million years — a slow rhythm, the heartbeat of stone.

They gathered in a place with no air, no ground, no sky.  
A place only mountains could stand:  
the **Horizon Between Worlds**.

And on this horizon, the greatest peaks of every world arrived.

---

## 🏔️ **Chapter 1: The Earthborn Elder**

Earth’s representative was **Chomolungma**, whom humans call **Everest**.

She was not the tallest in the council, but she was the oldest in spirit — carved by water, wind, ice, and time.  
She carried the memory of forests, the scent of rivers, the echo of human footsteps.

Everest bowed to the others, her snowy crown shimmering.





“I bring greetings from the world of breath,” she said.  
“Where mountains are climbed, feared, loved, and named.”

The other mountains murmured.  
Earth was strange to them — a world where beings so small dared to touch the sky.

---

## 🔥 **Chapter 2: The Martian Giant**

Then came **Olympus Mons**, the titan of Mars.

He was so tall that even the horizon bowed beneath him.  
A volcano the size of a country, a mountain so wide that a traveler could walk for weeks and never realize they were climbing.





Olympus Mons spoke with a voice like rumbling magma.

“I bring greetings from the world of dust,” he said.  
“Where storms circle the planet for months, and silence is the only wind.”

He looked down at Everest kindly.

“You are small,” he said, “but you are brave.”

Everest smiled.

---

## 🌋 **Chapter 3: The Venusian Peak**

From Venus came **Maxwell Montes**, shimmering with metallic frost.

She was tall — taller than Everest — but she carried herself lightly, as though gravity itself respected her.





“I bring greetings from the world of fire,” she said.  
“Where mountains glow beneath clouds of acid, and the air is heavy enough to crush dreams.”

The others bowed.  
Venus was a world of trials, and Maxwell Montes was its survivor.

---

## 🪐 **Chapter 4: The Asteroid Scar**

Then came **Rheasilvia Peak**, from the asteroid Vesta.

He was strange — not a mountain grown from stone, but a mountain born from violence.  
An impact had carved him upward, a scar taller than Everest three times over.





“I bring greetings from the world of wounds,” he said.  
“Where mountains are not raised, but torn upward by the hand of fate.”

Olympus Mons nodded solemnly.  
Even giants respected scars.

---

## 🌑 **Chapter 5: The Moon’s Whisper**

From Earth’s Moon came **Mons Huygens**, small compared to the others, but proud.

He stepped forward shyly.





“I bring greetings from the world of silence,” he said.  
“Where shadows last forever, and mountains watch the Earth rise.”

Everest touched her snowy forehead to his rocky one.

“You are family,” she whispered.

---

## 🌠 **Chapter 6: The Council Speaks**

The mountains gathered in a circle.

Olympus Mons towered above them.  
Maxwell Montes glowed softly.  
Rheasilvia Peak stood like a broken spear.  
Mons Huygens shimmered faintly.  
Everest breathed gently, like a mother calming children.

They spoke of:

- erosion  
- lava  
- frost  
- silence  
- storms  
- meteors  
- gravity  
- time  

And then they spoke of **humans**.

Everest told stories of climbers — tiny beings who carried fire in their hearts and dared to touch the sky.

Maxwell Montes listened with wonder.  
Rheasilvia Peak listened with envy.  
Mons Huygens listened with longing.  
Olympus Mons listened with pride.

Finally, Olympus Mons said:

“Perhaps one day, they will climb me.”

Maxwell Montes laughed softly.

“Perhaps one day, they will survive me.”

Rheasilvia Peak rumbled.

“Perhaps one day, they will understand me.”

Mons Huygens whispered:

“Perhaps one day, they will visit me.”

Everest smiled.

“They already have.”

The council fell silent.

Humans — tiny, fragile, brief — had touched the Moon.

The mountains of the solar system felt something like awe.

---

## 🌌 **Epilogue: The Next Council**

The mountains returned to their worlds.

Everest to her winds.  
Olympus Mons to his dust.  
Maxwell Montes to her fire.  
Rheasilvia Peak to his silence.  
Mons Huygens to his shadows.

And they waited.

For the next council.  
For the next thousand million years.  
For the next story humans might bring.

Because somewhere, on a small blue world, tiny beings were already dreaming of climbing mountains that no human had ever seen.

And the mountains — vast, ancient, patient — were waiting.
